# Type escape hatches in real TypeScript codebases: measurement report

The data behind `/research/type-escape-hatches`, measured on 2026-10-10. Generated tables are in `tables.md`. The machine-readable results are `rule-census.json`, `escape-hatch-census.json`, `linters.json`, `typed-census.json`, `sample.json` and `classifications.json`. Everything is regenerated by the scripts in `research/corpus/scripts/` (see §9).

At the time, Ultracite's Oxlint core preset enabled `typescript/no-explicit-any`, `typescript/no-non-null-assertion`, `typescript/ban-ts-comment`, `typescript/prefer-ts-expect-error`, `typescript/consistent-type-assertions` (default options) and the type-aware `typescript/no-unsafe-*` family, including `no-unsafe-type-assertion` and `no-unnecessary-type-assertion`. The type-aware rules only run with `--type-aware`. The ESLint preset enables every non-deprecated typescript-eslint rule with `projectService`, so its type-aware rules always run. Biome's core preset enables `noExplicitAny`, `noNonNullAssertion`, `noTsIgnore`, `noImplicitAnyLet`, `noEvolvingTypes`, `noExtraNonNullAssertion` and `noNonNullAssertedOptionalChain`, and turns `noExplicitAny` off in test files. The Oxlint and ESLint presets keep it on in tests (see §8).

## TL;DR

- **Corpus.** The same 8 codebases and 3,333 files (694,309 lines) as the cognitive complexity report, at the same SHAs. Ultracite's own CLI is reported separately.
- **Ultracite's Oxlint presets reproduce the preliminary numbers exactly.** With in-file suppressions honoured, `typescript/no-explicit-any` fires 3,840 times in 7 of 8 repos, `no-non-null-assertion` 1,378 times in 7 of 8, and `ban-ts-comment` 122 times in 4 of 8. Of 343 rules with any hits, `no-explicit-any` ranks 4th and `no-non-null-assertion` 11th.
- **Counting every occurrence** (suppressions neutralised): 4,292 `any` (61.8 per 10,000 lines) in 8 of 8 repos, 1,416 `!` (20.4 per 10,000) in 8 of 8, 5,375 type assertions (77.4 per 10,000), 106 `@ts-ignore`, 168 `@ts-expect-error` and no `@ts-nocheck`. There are 2,019 `unknown`, about one for every two `any`.
- **The rules are purely syntactic.** The AST count of `any` keywords and `!` expressions equals Oxlint's raw rule count in every repo. ESLint (typescript-eslint 8.71.0) matches Oxlint exactly on all three rules. Biome differs on 15 lines, for three explainable reasons (§5).
- **Repos that enforce the rules still use the hatches.** Hono suppresses 414 of its 434 `any`. VS Code's slice suppresses all 38 of its `any`, and tRPC all 38 of its `!`. Zod's config turns `noExplicitAny` off with the comment "`any` is amazing".
- **Where `any` sits.** 28.7% of `any` are in casts (1,173 casts to `any`, counting double casts), 18.2% are parameters and 10.7% are type-parameter constraints or defaults. 27.1% are generic arguments such as `Record<string, any>`.
- **Why maintainers wrote them** (96 `any` and 96 `!`, hand-classified, 12 per repo):
  - `any`: 35% generic plumbing, 21% untyped boundaries, 19% type-system workarounds, 8% error handling and 17% convenience (22% weighted by repo size). By the classifier's judgment, replacing `any` with `unknown` would very likely break the code in 54 of 96 cases, and very likely work in 27.
  - `!`: 49% are backed by a check TypeScript can't see, 22% by an invariant, 17% by a lifecycle step and 8% by an external contract. Only 4 of 96 (4%) are unguarded. Only 5 of 96 carry a comment saying why.
  - A blind second pass on 20 of each agreed on the category for 19 of 20 `any` (κ = 0.93) and 13 of 20 `!` (κ = 0.48). On the coarse split (convenience vs a reason; unguarded vs backed) it agreed on 19 and 20.
- **Type-aware view (zod and hono, which type-check without installing anything).**
  - 536 of 703 plain `as T` casts (76%) narrow the type, which is what `no-unsafe-type-assertion` flags. 97 (14%) are unnecessary.
  - 47 of zod's 95 `!` are unnecessary under zod's own tsconfig. 41 of those are on indexed access, and zod sets `noUncheckedIndexedAccess: false`.
  - The `no-unsafe-*` family fires 1,162 times in zod and 409 times in hono, downstream of 871 and 434 explicit `any`.
- **What no rule here catches.** 53 definite-assignment assertions (`field!: T`). Implicit `any` (`JSON.parse`) and member access on `any` are caught only by the type-aware rules. `as unknown as T` passes `no-explicit-any`; 156 double casts go through `unknown`.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Rule census (`rule-census.cjs`)

Oxlint ran twice per repo:

- **reported:** the files as they are, so in-file `eslint-disable` and `oxlint-disable` comments are honoured. This is what `ultracite check` would report.
- **raw:** a mirror with every `eslint-` or `oxlint-` `disable`/`enable` directive rewritten to the same length (`xxxxxx-disable`), so lines and columns don't move. This counts every occurrence. 444 directives were neutralised across the 9 repos.

Each diagnostic code (`typescript(no-explicit-any)`) is mapped to its config name (`typescript/no-explicit-any`). Two quirks: `jsx-a11y` contains digits, and Oxlint reports the `react/rules-of-hooks` and `react/exhaustive-deps` rules under a `react-hooks(...)` scope. The run fails if any diagnostic maps to a rule the presets don't enable, which is how the second quirk was found.

As a cross-check, the census's `complexity` count (601 pooled) equals the cognitive complexity report's count of functions over cyclomatic 20 (601).

### 3.2 Escape-hatch census (`escape-hatch-census.cjs`)

Every file is parsed with typescript-estree 8.71.0, syntax only. Each occurrence is classified by walking up its ancestors:

- **`any` and `unknown` by site:** cast, parameter, rest parameter, return type, variable annotation, type-parameter constraint or default, function type or method signature, interface or type-literal property, class property, index signature, catch parameter, conditional/mapped/indexed type, type alias, type argument at a call site, or extends/implements clause. The first match walking up wins, so the `any` in `x as Record<string, any>` is a cast.
- **`any` by form:** bare, `any[]`, generic argument, or union member.
- **`!` by what it asserts:** a `.get()` result, a regex match, a DOM lookup (`getElementById`, `querySelector`, `closest`, `getContext`), an array search or pop, an indexed access, a ref's `.current`, a field on `this`, another property, a variable or parameter, another call or another expression. Also by use: member access (`x!.y`), call (`f!()`) or value.
- **Assertions** (`as` and `<T>`) by kind: `as const`, `as any`, `as unknown`, `as never`, double (an assertion whose operand is an assertion, counted once, by its inner type) and other `as T`.
- **`@ts-*` comments**, using typescript-eslint's own directive patterns. "With a reason" means at least 3 characters after the directive, trimmed, which is how `ban-ts-comment` measures it. So `// @ts-expect-error - ??` has a reason (tRPC [`rsc.tsx` L110](https://github.com/trpc/trpc/blob/796700d8653b3832409fff997e6f5789519b9ea7/packages/react-query/src/rsc.tsx#L110)).
- **Suppression comments** (`eslint-disable*`, `oxlint-disable*`, `biome-ignore`) that name an escape-hatch rule.

The `any` and `!` totals equal Oxlint's raw `no-explicit-any` and `no-non-null-assertion` counts in all 9 repos. `ban-ts-comment`'s raw count equals the `@ts-ignore` count plus the `@ts-expect-error` comments without a reason, in all 9.

### 3.3 Three linters (`escape-hatch-linters.cjs`)

- **Calibration:** 36 hand-written cases in `calibration/escape-hatches/` (`cases.ts`, `nocheck.ts`, with a strict `tsconfig.json`) ran in Oxlint (core + react), in ESLint with the syntax-only and type-aware typescript-eslint rules, and in Biome.
- **Corpus:** the raw mirror ran through ESLint and Biome with the three syntax-only rules. Every line where Biome's count differs from the census is recorded with its code.

### 3.4 Typed census (`typed-census.cjs`)

Type-aware rules need types, and the clones have no `node_modules`. Zod and Hono have no runtime dependencies, so their sources can be type-checked as cloned. The script first type-checks the collected files with the TypeScript compiler API, using the repo's own root `tsconfig.json`, and records every diagnostic by code. A repo whose imports didn't resolve would be excluded rather than measured on error types.

- **zod:** 0 TypeScript diagnostics.
- **hono:** 17 diagnostics, all missing platform globals (`process`, Bun and Deno names: TS2304, TS2503, TS2591) and 4 implicit-`any` parameters (TS7006). There are no unresolved modules. Those few names become error types, which the `no-unsafe-*` rules treat as `any`.

ESLint then runs the type-aware rules with `projectService`. Each `no-unsafe-type-assertion` and `no-unnecessary-type-assertion` diagnostic is joined to an assertion or `!` site by exact start position. Every `no-unsafe-type-assertion` position (749 in zod, 368 in hono; a double cast can report twice at one position) and all 212 `no-unnecessary-type-assertion` diagnostics joined to a site. A `!` showing one "unsafe" in each repo is a position collision with an assertion that starts at the same character, not a real finding.

### 3.5 Sampling and classification (`sample-escape-hatches.cjs`, `classification-agreement.cjs`)

- **Sample.** Seed 20261010, 12 sites of each kind per external repo, drawn uniformly from that repo's census sites (every occurrence, suppressed or not). That gives 96 `any` and 96 `!`. Shares are reported unweighted and weighted by each repo's total.
- **Codebook.** Five categories per kind, stored in `classifications.json`:
  - `any`: generic plumbing, untyped boundary, type-system workaround, error handling, convenience.
  - `!`: checked nearby, lifecycle, invariant, external contract, unguarded.
  - Plus `acknowledged`, and either `unknownWouldDo` or `optionalChainSafe`.
- **First pass.** A Claude subagent per kind classified every site from the worksheet: 12 lines of context, plus the repo file, read-only, when needed. The `any` pass read about 30 files and the `!` pass about 50.
- **Second pass.** The lead agent (also Claude) re-labelled a seeded subset of 20 per kind blind. `--print` shows code only. Agreement:

| Kind | Same category | Cohen's κ | Same coarse label | Coarse κ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `any` | 19 of 20 | 0.93 | 19 of 20 | 0.83 |
| `!` | 13 of 20 | 0.48 | 20 of 20 | 1.00 |

All seven `!` disagreements were the first pass's `checked-nearby` against the second's `lifecycle` or `invariant`. The first pass had read more of each file, and those categories overlap: a check in other code, made as part of a known call sequence, fits all three. The coarse split (unguarded or not) is what the post relies on, and it agreed fully. Both raters are Claude models, so this measures how consistently the codebook can be applied, not human judgment.

Every Biome difference is explained in `tables.md` §7:

- **`T extends any`.** Biome skips a bare `any` constraint, a common `.tsx` idiom for generic arrow functions (4 in Excalidraw).
- **`x!!`.** Biome counts it once and typescript-eslint counts it twice (5 in Excalidraw).
- **Trailing comments.** Biome misses a `@ts-ignore` that follows code on the same line, such as `? // @ts-ignore` (6 in Next.js).
- **Narrower scope.** `noTsIgnore` only covers `@ts-ignore`, so the 27 `@ts-expect-error` comments without a reason, which `ban-ts-comment` flags, aren't in Biome's count.

## 6. Findings worth stating carefully

- **The syntax rules can't tell a reasoned `any` from a lazy one.** About 80% of sampled `any` had a reason, and in more than half of all cases `unknown` was not a drop-in replacement. The rule flags them all equally.
- **`!` is almost always backed by something** (96%), and almost never documented (5%). The rule's cost here is restructuring code that was correct, or adding a suppression that says why.
- **The rules don't see the hatches that remain.** `as unknown as T` (156), plain narrowing casts (76% of plain casts in zod and hono), definite assignment (53) and implicit `any` are all invisible to the syntax-only rules. Only the type-aware rules see the casts.

## 7. Caveats

- **Snapshot.** Each repo is at its default-branch HEAD as pinned on 2026-10-10. Next.js is 40% of the pooled lines.
- **Libraries, not apps.** Seven of the eight are libraries or frameworks with heavy generic APIs. Application code may use `any` differently; Bogner and Merkel's apps averaged more.
- **Syntax only, except §4.7.** The census can't see implicit `any` or what a cast does. The typed census covers two repos, chosen because they type-check without dependencies, and both are among the heaviest `any` users. Hono's 17 environment diagnostics make a few names error types.
- **The classification is judgment.** It was made by LLM raters with a written codebook, from 12 lines of context and the file. The `!` categories overlap (κ = 0.48), but the coarse split doesn't. `unknownWouldDo` and `optionalChainSafe` are the classifier's estimates, not compiled.
- **Equal allocation.** Small repos are over-represented in the unweighted shares, so weighted shares are given too.
- **"Reported" honours in-file suppressions only.** Repo-level config such as tRPC turning `no-explicit-any` off isn't applied, because Ultracite's presets replace it.

## 8. Notes on Ultracite itself

- **Preset inconsistency.** Biome's core preset turns `noExplicitAny` off for test files (added in `b4ba620a`, August 2025, with no recorded reason). The Oxlint and ESLint presets keep `no-explicit-any` on in tests. Raised with Hayden rather than written around; the post doesn't mention test files.
- **History.** `no-explicit-any`, `no-non-null-assertion` and `ban-ts-comment` were errors in the project's first commit (`e403fb15`, 18 March 2022, when it was the `harmony` ESLint config, "Unified, strict editor configuration for modern Next.js apps"). They carried through the Biome (#140) and Oxlint presets. No issue or commit records a specific rationale.
- **The agent handoff** forbids lint-suppression comments (`oxlint-disable`, `eslint-disable`, `biome-ignore`), but not `@ts-ignore` or `@ts-expect-error` by name. In Ultracite's presets, those are caught by `ban-ts-comment` in the re-lint, except a `@ts-expect-error` with a reason.
- **Oxlint's help text** for `no-non-null-assertion` suggests `?.`. That fits only the 30% of `!` that are dereferenced (§4.4), and in the sample it would have changed behavior in two cases.

## 9. Reproducing

From the repo root:

```sh
bash research/corpus/scripts/clone.sh
T=type-escape-hatches
node research/corpus/scripts/rule-census.cjs --topic $T --focus typescript/no-explicit-any,typescript/no-non-null-assertion,typescript/ban-ts-comment
node research/corpus/scripts/escape-hatch-census.cjs --topic $T
node --max-old-space-size=8192 research/corpus/scripts/escape-hatch-linters.cjs --topic $T --calibration --corpus
node --max-old-space-size=8192 research/corpus/scripts/typed-census.cjs --topic $T --repos zod,hono
node research/corpus/scripts/sample-escape-hatches.cjs --topic $T
node research/corpus/scripts/escape-hatch-tables.cjs --topic $T
```

The whole pipeline takes under a minute. The sample is deterministic, and `classifications.json` holds the hand labels, so `tables.md` regenerates exactly. Re-running reproduces every file byte for byte, apart from the timing fields (`seconds`) in `rule-census.json` and `typed-census.json` and Ultracite's own SHA. To redo the agreement check, run `classification-agreement.cjs --print` and label blind, then `--compute`.
